Projections and distances

Most H2 distance questions come down to one right-angled triangle. The dot product gives you one side and the cross product gives you the other.

The picture

Take a line through A with direction d, and a point P that isn't on it. Drop a perpendicular from P to the line, and call where it lands F, the foot of the perpendicular. Triangle AFP has a right angle at F.

  • AF, the side along the line, is the length of projection of AP onto the line:  AF = |AP · d̂|
  • PF, the side across, is the perpendicular distance from P to the line:  PF = |AP × d̂|
  • Pythagoras links them:  AF² + PF² = |AP|²

Here d̂ = d / |d| is the unit vector. If you forget to divide by |d|, your answer will be |d| times too big.

Finding the foot of the perpendicular

This is the standard H2 method. It's worth knowing even though the formulas above give the distance directly.

  1. F lies on the line, so OF = a + λd for some λ.
  2. Write PF = OF − OP in terms of λ.
  3. PF is perpendicular to the line, so PF · d = 0. Solve this for λ.
  4. Substitute λ back in to get F. The distance is then |PF|.

To reflect P in the line, use the fact that F is the midpoint of P and its image P′, so OP′ = 2OF − OP.

Notice the pattern. To measure along a direction, you dot with a unit vector. A plane's normal points straight across to the plane, so distance to a plane uses the dot product with n̂. Distance to a line has no single "across" direction, which is why it uses the cross product.

Worked example

The line l has equation r = (1, 2, 3) + λ(2, −1, 1), and P is the point (4, 3, 5). Find the length of projection of AP onto l, the perpendicular distance from P to l, and the foot of the perpendicular.

Show solution
  1. A = (1, 2, 3), so AP = (3, 1, 2). Also |d| = √(4 + 1 + 1) = √6.
  2. AP · d = 6 − 1 + 2 = 7, so the length of projection = 7/√6 = 7√6/6 ≈ 2.86
  3. AP × d = ((1)(1) − (2)(−1), (2)(2) − (3)(1), (3)(−1) − (1)(2)) = (3, 1, −5), and |(3, 1, −5)| = √35
  4. Perpendicular distance = √35 / √6 = √210/6 ≈ 2.42
  5. Foot: AF = 7/√6 along d̂, so OF = a + (7/6)d = (10/3, 5/6, 25/6)
  6. Check with Pythagoras: 49/6 + 35/6 = 84/6 = 14 = |AP|² ✓

These are the starting values in the demo above.

Common mistakes
  • Using d instead of d̂. Divide by |d|.
  • Using the position vector OP instead of AP. The vector must go from a point on the line to P.
  • Dotting when you should cross, or the other way round. Distance to a plane uses the dot product with the normal. Distance to a line uses the cross product with the direction.
