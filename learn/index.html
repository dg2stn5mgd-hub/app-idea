The dot product (scalar product)

The dot product takes two vectors and gives you a single number. That number tells you how much the two vectors point the same way.

The two formulas

For a = (a₁, a₂, a₃) and b = (b₁, b₂, b₃), there are two ways to work out a · b, and they always give the same answer:

a · b = a₁b₁ + a₂b₂ + a₃b₃
a · b = |a||b| cos θ

Here θ is the angle between a and b when they're drawn from the same point, with 0° ≤ θ ≤ 180°. The first formula is how you calculate it. The second is what it means. Put them together and you get the angle formula:

cos θ = (a · b) / (|a||b|)

Try it

Move the sliders and watch the sign of a · b. When the angle is acute it's positive, at exactly 90° it's zero, and when the angle is obtuse it's negative.

Dot product explorer

Key facts to remember

  • a · b is a scalar, not a vector.
  • Perpendicular test: for non-zero vectors, a · b = 0 ⇔ a ⊥ b.
  • a · a = |a|². This is useful for working out lengths.
  • Order doesn't matter: a · b = b · a. It also expands like normal brackets: a · (b + c) = a · b + a · c.
  • Sign of a · b: positive means θ is acute, zero means θ = 90°, negative means θ is obtuse.

Where it shows up in H2

  • Angle between two vectors: cos θ = (a · b)/(|a||b|).
  • Angle between two lines: use the directions, with a modulus to get the acute angle: cos θ = |d₁ · d₂|/(|d₁||d₂|).
  • Length of projection of a onto b: |a · b̂|, where b̂ = b/|b|.
  • Projection vector of a onto b: (a · b̂)b̂.
  • Foot of perpendicular: the key step is always "this vector is perpendicular to the line, so the dot product is 0".
  • Equation of a plane: r · n = D says that every point on the plane gives the same dot product with the normal.

Worked example

Given a = 122 and b = 2−12, find the angle between a and b, and the length of projection of a onto b.

Show solution
  1. a · b = (1)(2) + (2)(−1) + (2)(2) = 2 − 2 + 4 = 4
  2. |a| = √(1 + 4 + 4) = 3, and |b| = √(4 + 1 + 4) = 3
  3. cos θ = 4 / (3 × 3) = 4/9, so θ = cos⁻¹(4/9) ≈ 63.6°
  4. Length of projection = |a · b| / |b| = 4/3

Check it yourself: set these vectors in the explorer above.

Common mistakes
  • Writing the answer to a · b as a vector. It's always a single number.
  • Forgetting the modulus when the question asks for the acute angle between two lines or planes.
  • Using b instead of the unit vector b̂ when finding a projection. Remember to divide by |b|.
  • Saying "a · b = 0 so they're parallel". Zero means perpendicular.

The cross product (vector product)

The cross product takes two vectors and gives you a new vector that is perpendicular to both of them. Its length equals the area of the parallelogram they make.

The formula

a × b = a₁a₂a₃× b₁b₂b₃= a₂b₃ − a₃b₂a₃b₁ − a₁b₃a₁b₂ − a₂b₁

How to remember it: to get a row of the answer, cover that row in a and b and cross-multiply the two rows that are left. The top row gives a₂b₃ − a₃b₂, and the bottom row gives a₁b₂ − a₂b₁. The middle row is where most mistakes happen. It's a₃b₁ − a₁b₃, which is the opposite way round from the pattern, so many students write the cross-multiplication and then flip its sign.

What it means

  • Direction: perpendicular to both a and b, following the right-hand rule. Point your fingers along a and curl them towards b; your thumb points along a × b.
  • Length: |a × b| = |a||b| sin θ, which is the area of the parallelogram with sides a and b.

Try it

Cross product explorer

Key facts to remember

  • a × b is a vector, unlike the dot product.
  • Order matters: b × a = −(a × b).
  • Parallel test: for non-zero vectors, a × b = 0 ⇔ a ∥ b. In particular, a × a = 0.
  • (a × b) · a = 0 and (a × b) · b = 0. Use this to check your answer.
  • Brackets expand normally: a × (b + c) = a × b + a × c. Just keep the order.

Where it shows up in H2

  • Normal to a plane: if a plane contains directions b and c, then n = b × c. This is how you convert r = a + λb + μc into r · n = D.
  • Area of a triangle ABC = ½ |AB × AC|. The area of a parallelogram is |a × b|.
  • Line where two planes meet: its direction is n₁ × n₂.
  • Perpendicular distance from a point to a line: |AP × d̂|. See Projections.
  • Shortest distance between skew lines: project onto d₁ × d₂, the direction perpendicular to both lines.

Worked example

A(1, 0, 2), B(3, 1, 1) and C(2, −1, 4). Find a Cartesian equation of the plane ABC and the area of triangle ABC.

Show solution
  1. AB = (2, 1, −1) and AC = (1, −1, 2)
  2. n = AB × AC = ((1)(2) − (−1)(−1), (−1)(1) − (2)(2), (2)(−1) − (1)(1)) = (1, −5, −3)
  3. Check: n · AB = 2 − 5 + 3 = 0 ✓, and n · AC = 1 + 5 − 6 = 0 ✓
  4. D = n · OA = 1 + 0 − 6 = −5, so the plane is x − 5y − 3z = −5
  5. Area = ½ |n| = ½ √(1 + 25 + 9) = ½√35 ≈ 2.96 units²
Common mistakes
  • Getting the sign of the middle component wrong. Always check (a × b) · a = 0.
  • Swapping the order and not noticing. That's fine for a normal vector, because any multiple works, but not when the direction matters.
  • Forgetting the ½ for the area of a triangle.
  • Mixing up the tests: a · b = 0 means perpendicular, and a × b = 0 means parallel.

Projections and distances

Most H2 distance questions come down to one right-angled triangle. The dot product gives you one side and the cross product gives you the other.

The picture

Take a line through A with direction d, and a point P that isn't on it. Drop a perpendicular from P to the line, and call where it lands F, the foot of the perpendicular. Triangle AFP has a right angle at F.

  • AF, the side along the line, is the length of projection of AP onto the line:  AF = |AP · d̂|
  • PF, the side across, is the perpendicular distance from P to the line:  PF = |AP × d̂|
  • Pythagoras links them:  AF² + PF² = |AP|²

Here d̂ = d / |d| is the unit vector. If you forget to divide by |d|, your answer will be |d| times too big.

Try it

Point and line

Finding the foot of the perpendicular

This is the standard H2 method. It's worth knowing even though the formulas above give the distance directly.

  1. F lies on the line, so OF = a + λd for some λ.
  2. Write PF = OF − OP in terms of λ.
  3. PF is perpendicular to the line, so PF · d = 0. Solve this for λ.
  4. Substitute λ back in to get F. The distance is then |PF|.

To reflect P in the line, use the fact that F is the midpoint of P and its image P′, so OP′ = 2OF − OP.

Notice the pattern. To measure along a direction, you dot with a unit vector. A plane's normal points straight across to the plane, so distance to a plane uses the dot product with n̂. Distance to a line has no single "across" direction, which is why it uses the cross product.

Worked example

The line l has equation r = (1, 2, 3) + λ(2, −1, 1), and P is the point (4, 3, 5). Find the length of projection of AP onto l, the perpendicular distance from P to l, and the foot of the perpendicular.

Show solution
  1. A = (1, 2, 3), so AP = (3, 1, 2). Also |d| = √(4 + 1 + 1) = √6.
  2. AP · d = 6 − 1 + 2 = 7, so the length of projection = 7/√6 = 7√6/6 ≈ 2.86
  3. AP × d = ((1)(1) − (2)(−1), (2)(2) − (3)(1), (3)(−1) − (1)(2)) = (3, 1, −5), and |(3, 1, −5)| = √35
  4. Perpendicular distance = √35 / √6 = √210/6 ≈ 2.42
  5. Foot: AF = 7/√6 along d̂, so OF = a + (7/6)d = (10/3, 5/6, 25/6)
  6. Check with Pythagoras: 49/6 + 35/6 = 84/6 = 14 = |AP|² ✓

These are the starting values in the demo above.

Common mistakes
  • Using d instead of d̂. Divide by |d|.
  • Using the position vector OP instead of AP. The vector must go from a point on the line to P.
  • Dotting when you should cross, or the other way round. Distance to a plane uses the dot product with the normal. Distance to a line uses the cross product with the direction.
