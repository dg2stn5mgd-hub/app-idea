The dot product (scalar product)

The dot product takes two vectors and gives you a single number. That number tells you how much the two vectors point the same way.

The two formulas

For a = (a₁, a₂, a₃) and b = (b₁, b₂, b₃), there are two ways to work out a · b, and they always give the same answer:

a · b = a₁b₁ + a₂b₂ + a₃b₃
a · b = |a||b| cos θ

Here θ is the angle between a and b when they're drawn from the same point, with 0° ≤ θ ≤ 180°. The first formula is how you calculate it. The second is what it means. Put them together and you get the angle formula:

cos θ = (a · b) / (|a||b|)

Key facts to remember

  • a · b is a scalar, not a vector.
  • Perpendicular test: for non-zero vectors, a · b = 0 ⇔ a ⊥ b.
  • a · a = |a|². This is useful for working out lengths.
  • Order doesn't matter: a · b = b · a. It also expands like normal brackets: a · (b + c) = a · b + a · c.
  • Sign of a · b: positive means θ is acute, zero means θ = 90°, negative means θ is obtuse.

Where it shows up in H2

  • Angle between two vectors: cos θ = (a · b)/(|a||b|).
  • Angle between two lines: use the directions, with a modulus to get the acute angle: cos θ = |d₁ · d₂|/(|d₁||d₂|).
  • Length of projection of a onto b: |a · b̂|, where b̂ = b/|b|.
  • Projection vector of a onto b: (a · b̂)b̂.
  • Foot of perpendicular: the key step is always "this vector is perpendicular to the line, so the dot product is 0".
  • Equation of a plane: r · n = D says that every point on the plane gives the same dot product with the normal.

Worked example

Given a = 122 and b = 2−12, find the angle between a and b, and the length of projection of a onto b.

Show solution
  1. a · b = (1)(2) + (2)(−1) + (2)(2) = 2 − 2 + 4 = 4
  2. |a| = √(1 + 4 + 4) = 3, and |b| = √(4 + 1 + 4) = 3
  3. cos θ = 4 / (3 × 3) = 4/9, so θ = cos⁻¹(4/9) ≈ 63.6°
  4. Length of projection = |a · b| / |b| = 4/3

Common mistakes
  • Writing the answer to a · b as a vector. It's always a single number.
  • Forgetting the modulus when the question asks for the acute angle between two lines or planes.
  • Using b instead of the unit vector b̂ when finding a projection. Remember to divide by |b|.
  • Saying "a · b = 0 so they're parallel". Zero means perpendicular.
